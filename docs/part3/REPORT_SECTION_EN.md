# Smart Contract Design, Authorization and Security Evaluation

The prototype separates authority and business logic across three interacting contracts. `RoleRegistry` establishes a fixed administrator and permits one-time self-registration as Customer or Provider. `EscrowOrders` owns the order state machine, accepts exact-value deposits, accounts for locked and claimable liabilities, and implements pull withdrawals. `DisputeResolver` records dispute hashes and evidence events, checks the fixed administrator through the registry, and invokes restricted dispute and settlement entry points on `EscrowOrders`. The resolver can be configured only once, and `EscrowOrders` verifies that the candidate resolver points back to the same orders and registry contracts. This prevents an administrator from accidentally binding an unrelated deployment.

Authorization is enforced on chain rather than trusted to the user interface. Only a registered Customer can create an order; only its nominated Provider can accept and submit; only its Customer can fund, approve, cancel or claim an overdue refund; either participant may open a dispute and add evidence; and only the fixed administrator may resolve an active dispute. Direct calls to `markDisputed` and `resolveDispute` are restricted to the bound resolver. Public registration cannot grant Administrator or overwrite an existing role.

The financial design uses pull payments. Funding increases `lockedTotal`; settlement reduces locked liabilities, increases `claimableTotal`, and credits exactly one beneficiary. Withdrawal follows checks-effects-interactions: the caller's credit and aggregate claimable liability are cleared before the external transfer, and a reentrancy lock prevents a callback from withdrawing twice. A failed recipient transfer reverts the transaction and therefore preserves the credit. Under normal operation, the contract balance equals `lockedTotal + claimableTotal`; unsolicited forced ETH may make the balance larger, so the strict safety invariant is balance greater than or equal to recorded liabilities.

Fourteen automated tests passed locally on 13 October 2026. They cover role escalation, repeated registration, administrator-only immutable resolver configuration, zero/EOA/cross-system bindings, invalid order creation, exact funding, the successful release path, both dispute outcomes, participant-only evidence, nonzero evidence hashes, cross-contract event emission, cancellation, deadline refund, illegal transitions, repeated settlement, rejecting recipients, reentrancy, multiple simultaneous orders and final liability reconciliation. Existing Sepolia evidence additionally records successful deployment and resolver binding, repository bytecode matching, 32 successful MetaMask business transactions, and order- and contract-level fund reconciliation. Local tests and public-chain evidence are deliberately reported separately.

Gas optimization was evaluated by executing the same deployment and representative lifecycle twice on a deterministic Ganache/Shanghai chain with solc 0.8.30. Compared with optimizer disabled, optimizer 200 reduced deployment gas by 39.4% for `RoleRegistry`, 48.6% for `EscrowOrders`, and 48.7% for `DisputeResolver`; runtime bytecode sizes fell by approximately one half. Representative business transactions improved by 0.9% to 6.1%. These measurements support the repository's current optimizer-200 configuration. They are local comparative measurements rather than Sepolia or mainnet fee quotations, and smart-account forwarding overhead is outside the contract-only figures.

The principal residual risk is governance, not an identified arithmetic or reentrancy defect: one fixed administrator has final authority over subjective disputes, with no appeal, pause, upgrade or multisignature mechanism. On-chain hashes prove that retrieved metadata is unchanged but do not prove its truth or guarantee off-chain availability. Addresses, amounts and hashes are public. Compromised user or administrator keys cannot be recovered by the contracts. Consequently, the system is suitable as a testnet course prototype but is not represented as production-grade or independently audited.
